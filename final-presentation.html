Slide 5 · Strategy

Strategy

Problem

Cinephiles leave StreamLine to discover films on Letterboxd, IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes, and MUBI, driving churn and reducing StreamLine's relevance as a discovery platform.

Value Proposition

A curated discovery experience that explains why a film is worth watching, helping users choose confidently without leaving StreamLine.

Hypothesis

We believe "Why You'll Love This" for discovery-driven viewers will increase Discovery-to-Play Conversion by helping them confidently choose a film, measured by Discovery-to-Play Conversion within 14 days while protecting the 30+ Minute Session Rate.

Slide 6 · Research

Research

Users discover films on Letterboxd, IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes, and MUBI because they trust expert curation more than StreamLine's recommendations.
Open StreamLine → Browse recommendations → Hesitate due to repetitive suggestions → Leave for Letterboxd/IMDb/MUBI (moment of misery) → Return to StreamLine after choosing a film elsewhere → Spotlight intervenes with curated explanations at the decision moment.

Slide 8 · Validation

Validation

A/B Test (50/50 split, 14 days)

Control: Current discovery experience with repetitive recommendations.

Variant: Spotlight + Curated Rail + Hidden Gems + "Why You'll Love This" explanations.

Primary Metric: Discovery-to-Play Conversion | Baseline: 34% | MDE: +2 pts (34% → 36%)

Guardrail: 30+ Minute Session Rate (baseline 11%, must not drop >2 pts)

Ship: ≥ +2 pts lift, p < 0.05
Iterate: Positive trend but below MDE
Kill: No lift or negative impact

Slide 10 · Story

Story

Friction: Reducing a broad discovery problem into one measurable feature without expanding scope into personalization or recommendation engines.

Aha Moment: StreamLine's competitor is not another streaming platform, it's the place where users decide what to watch: Letterboxd, IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes, and MUBI.

Takeaways

  • Discovery is a trust problem, not an algorithm problem.
  • Clear metrics eliminate scope creep.
  • MVPs should focus on one behavior change.
